Motivation

The old and deteriorating bridge network in Bangladesh is at risk due to increasing traffic density, environmental pollution, and climatic risks. Conventional visual inspection techniques are biased, time-consuming, and unable to identify internal damage at an early stage. This motivated my interest in developing intelligent, data-driven approaches to structural health monitoring (SHM) that offer continuous, automated, and reliable damage assessment of infrastructure.

Past work

My undergraduate thesis at BUET, supervised by Prof. Shohel Rana, focused on developing a deep learning-based framework for bridge damage identification using vehicle-induced dynamic displacement data. I modeled the problem as binary classification — presence or absence of structural damage — from vibration response signals. A key challenge was the lack of labeled damage data, a common bottleneck in real-world SHM. To address this, I implemented a semi-supervised model based on a Multilayer Perceptron (MLP), using both labeled and unlabeled data to improve detection performance. I also explored Bi-directional LSTM networks to capture temporal dependencies in vibration signals, which improved sensitivity to subtle damage patterns. This work was presented at IUT-ICCET and published as a first-author paper in the IUT Journal of Engineering and Technology (2024). See the Publications page for the full citation and PDF.

Ongoing work

I am currently writing a follow-up paper on damage localization — extending the detection framework not just to flag damage, but to locate it within the structure, by extracting spatial information from multi-sensor vibration data.

Broader interests

Beyond SHM, I am interested in developing risk assessment frameworks for infrastructure in Bangladesh, in continued collaboration between RHD and BUET, and in Intelligent Transportation Systems (ITS) as a related application of data-driven modeling to civil infrastructure.
