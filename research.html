Research

Structural Health Monitoring · Machine Learning for Infrastructure

Motivation

The old and deteriorating bridge network in Bangladesh is at risk from increasing traffic density, environmental pollution, and climatic hazards. Conventional visual inspection is subjective, slow, and unable to identify internal damage at an early stage. That gap is what drew me to structural health monitoring (SHM): intelligent, data-driven methods that can assess infrastructure continuously and automatically rather than through periodic manual survey.

Past work

My undergraduate thesis at BUET, supervised by Prof. Shohel Rana, developed a deep learning framework for bridge damage identification from vehicle-induced dynamic displacement data. I modeled the task as binary classification — damage present or absent — on vibration response signals.

The hardest constraint was labels. Confirmed cases of structural damage are rare, which is the standard bottleneck in real-world SHM. I addressed it with a semi-supervised model based on a Multilayer Perceptron, learning from labeled and unlabeled data together, and also explored Bi-directional LSTM networks to capture temporal dependencies in the vibration signal, which improved sensitivity to subtle damage patterns. The work was presented at IUT-ICCET and published as a first-author paper; see Publications for the citation and PDF.

Ongoing work

Bridge damage localization from multi-sensor vibration data

Follow-up to the undergraduate thesis · manuscript in preparation

Extending the detection framework from binary classification toward locating damage within the structure, by extracting spatial information across multiple sensors rather than treating the response as a single aggregate signal.

Road damage detection using deep learning (RDD2022 benchmark)

Supervisor: Dr. Celia Shahnaz · target journal: IEEE Transactions on Intelligent Transportation Systems

A YOLOv8-based multi-national road damage detection and classification framework, benchmarked on the RDD2022 dataset (47,420 road images across six countries) and targeting improvement over the YOLOv5 baseline of mAP@0.5 = 0.563 reported in Construction and Building Materials (Elsevier, Q1, 2024).

Facility management data analytics (FMUCD dataset)

Reviewed by: Dr. Kahyun Jeon, Illinois Institute of Technology

Exploratory data analysis and quantitative diagnostics on a 3.7-million-record facility maintenance dataset covering twelve North American universities, producing a reproducible analysis notebook and a research proposal on predictive maintenance classification. The analysis was independently reviewed and positively assessed by Dr. Kahyun Jeon.

Where this is going

The three projects are versions of one question asked in different settings: how to infer the condition of physical infrastructure from indirect, noisy, sparsely labeled measurements. I would like to keep working on that in a doctoral program — on bridges through vibration data, on road networks through image data, and on built assets through maintenance records — and to build risk assessment frameworks that are usable for aging infrastructure in Bangladesh.
