Motivation

The old and deteriorating bridge network in Bangladesh is at risk due to increasing traffic density, environmental pollution, and climatic risks. Conventional visual inspection techniques are biased, time-consuming, and unable to identify internal damage at an early stage. This motivated my interest in developing intelligent, data-driven approaches to structural health monitoring (SHM) that offer continuous, automated, and reliable damage assessment of infrastructure.

Past work

My undergraduate thesis at BUET, supervised by Prof. Shohel Rana, focused on developing a deep learning-based framework for bridge damage identification using vehicle-induced dynamic displacement data. I modeled the problem as binary classification — presence or absence of structural damage — from vibration response signals. A key challenge was the lack of labeled damage data, a common bottleneck in real-world SHM. To address this, I implemented a semi-supervised model based on a Multilayer Perceptron (MLP), using both labeled and unlabeled data to improve detection performance. I also explored Bi-directional LSTM networks to capture temporal dependencies in vibration signals, which improved sensitivity to subtle damage patterns. This work was presented at IUT-ICCET and published as a first-author paper in the IUT Journal of Engineering and Technology (2024). See the Publications page for the full citation and PDF.

Ongoing work

Bridge damage localization from multi-sensor vibration data

Follow-up to undergraduate thesis, in preparation.

Extending the detection framework above from binary damage classification toward locating damage within the structure, using spatial information across multiple vibration sensors.

Road Damage Detection Using Deep Learning (RDD2022 benchmark)

Supervisor: Dr. Celia Shahnaz

Developing a YOLOv8-based multi-national road damage detection and classification framework, benchmarked on the RDD2022 dataset (47,420 road images, 6 countries), targeting improvement over the YOLOv5 baseline (mAP@0.5 = 0.563) reported in Construction and Building Materials (Elsevier, Q1, 2024). Approved by Dr. Celia Shahnaz; target journal: IEEE Transactions on Intelligent Transportation Systems.

Facility Management Data Analytics (FMUCD dataset)

Reviewed by: Dr. Kahyun Jeon, Illinois Institute of Technology

Conducted exploratory data analysis and quantitative diagnostics on a 3.7-million-record facility maintenance dataset spanning 12 North American universities, producing a reproducible analysis notebook and a research proposal on predictive maintenance classification. Independently reviewed and positively assessed by Dr. Kahyun Jeon (IIT).

Broader interests

Beyond SHM, I am interested in developing risk assessment frameworks for infrastructure in Bangladesh, in continued collaboration between RHD and BUET, and — as the projects above reflect — in applying data-driven and deep learning methods more broadly across civil infrastructure: Intelligent Transportation Systems (ITS), road condition monitoring, and facility/asset management analytics.
